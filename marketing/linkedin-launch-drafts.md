## 1. The positioning

Tell me what you do now and what you'd like to have happen, and I can help you get there.

That's the simplest way I've found to explain what I do.

You don't need to arrive with a software specification. You don't need to know
whether the answer is an integration, better reporting, AI, or a clearer handoff.

Start with the work:

- What's happening today?
- What would you like to happen instead?
- What keeps getting in the way?

My job is to make sense of the moving parts and help map a practical path between
those two points.

Sometimes that opens up a possibility you hadn't considered. But that shouldn't
mean losing sight of the problem you actually wanted solved.

I wrote up how I approach that path, including a one-page brief you can use
before talking to a technology partner:

https://rinsesoft.com/resources/business-problem-roadmap

## 2. A roadmap isn't a software shopping list

"We need a dashboard."

Maybe. But what would you like to happen when you have it?

If the answer is "I want my team to know which jobs need attention without asking
me," that's a much better starting point.

Now we can look at where the information lives, whether it's current, who
maintains it, and why the team can't act on it today.

The first change might not be a new screen. It might be agreeing what a job
status means and who is responsible for updating it.

Technology comes after we understand the gap.

I've put together a practical guide to turning a business problem into a
roadmap, with an illustrative customer-update workflow:

https://rinsesoft.com/resources/business-problem-roadmap

## 3. Start with one thing that keeps coming back to you

A customer wants an update.

Someone checks a spreadsheet. Someone searches an email thread. Then someone
asks you what was agreed.

Nothing is technically broken. But giving one reliable answer takes three people
and your attention.

Before buying another tool, trace one recent example:

Where did the work wait?
What information was missing?
Which decision could only you make?

Sometimes the fix is a clearer handoff. Sometimes it's connecting the tools you
already use. The point is to understand why the work comes back to you before
deciding what to build.

Here's a short, ungated guide to working through it:

https://rinsesoft.com/owner-bottlenecks
