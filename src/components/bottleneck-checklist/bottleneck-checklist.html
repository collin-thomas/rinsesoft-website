1. Where did it wait?

    Was someone waiting for a decision, information, or access? Ask what they needed to move forward, and who had it.

  2. What had to be done twice?

    Look for re-entered information, repeated checks, and corrections. Is the same work happening in more than one place?

  3. Why did it need you?

    Was your judgment genuinely needed, or were you the only person who knew the answer, had permission, or could find the information?

  4. What did the interruption cost?

    Note the people involved, time spent, and any delay or rework. Use observations rather than guessing at savings.

Keep customer details and sensitive records inside your business. A plain-language description of the pattern is enough for an initial conversation.
